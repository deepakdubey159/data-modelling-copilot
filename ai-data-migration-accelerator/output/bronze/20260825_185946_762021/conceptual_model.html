5Business Rules

Rules the structure and value distributions imply.

  1. 1

    A customer must have a credit limit that governs their purchasing authority.

  2. 2

    Products are assigned to a single category and sourced from a single supplier.

  3. 3

    A product may be promoted through zero or more promotions; promotions apply to one or more products.

  4. 4

    Inventory is tracked as the quantity of each product held in each warehouse, with a reorder level threshold to trigger replenishment.

  5. 5

    An order is placed by a customer and managed by a sales representative (employee); the order header captures the order date and status.

  6. 6

    An order comprises one or more line items, each specifying a product, quantity, and unit price at time of order.

  7. 7

    A payment is applied to an order; an order may have zero or one payment recorded (or multiple payments, depending on fulfillment state).

  8. 8

    A shipment originates from a warehouse and fulfills an order; shipments carry tracking numbers.

  9. 9

    A return can be initiated against an order and a specific product with a documented reason.

  10. 10

    An employee reports to a manager (another employee) within a department; not all employees have managers (they may be top-level).

  11. 11

    Customers and warehouses are located in cities; cities are in states; states are in countries.

6Assumptions

Where interpretation happened. Each of these is a judgement, not a fact read from the database, and should be confirmed.

  1. 1

    The business key for Customer is customer_number (a formatted identifier like 'CUST001') rather than the surrogate customer_id, as it is the human-facing identifier.

  2. 2

    The business key for Product is sku rather than product_id, as SKU is the universal product code in retail.

  3. 3

    The business key for Promotion is promotion_name, as promotions are identified by their campaign name in business operations.

  4. 4

    The business key for Shipment is tracking_number, as this is how customers and logistics teams identify a shipment in the real world.

  5. 5

    Payment is treated as an entity despite having an optional relationship to Order; it is assumed that payments are recorded independently and may be matched to orders later or in a different process.

  6. 6

    Audit Log is included as an entity though it is orphaned (no foreign keys reference it). It captures system operations and is part of the operational data model.

  7. 7

    The relationship from Product to Promotion is many-to-many; the junction table product_promotion is a pure link with no additional business attributes, so it is not exposed as a separate entity but instead modeled as a many-to-many relationship.

  8. 8

    Inventory is modeled as a business entity (not merely a junction table) because it carries business attributes (quantity and reorder_level) beyond the simple product-warehouse link.

  9. 9

    Order Line Item is treated as a full entity because it captures order-specific pricing and quantity distinct from the product's unit price.

  10. 10

    Return Order is assumed to be initiated after an order is placed; the structure suggests one return per return_id, but a business rule likely limits returns to valid order line items.

  11. 11

    Payment method and payment date are attributes of Payment; the amount represents what was paid and may differ from the order total if partial payments are supported.

  12. 12

    Employees can be sales representatives; those with sales_rep_id on orders are the Employee entities in a sales role.

  13. 13

    Department assignment is optional for employees, implying some employees may be unassigned or in a pending state.

  14. 14

    Customer city assignment is optional, allowing customers without a recorded location.

  15. 15

    Warehouse city assignment is optional, though unusual; warehouses may be in a setup or unknown-location state.

  16. 16

    All geographic rollups (Country → State → City) are mandatory in the hierarchy; customers and warehouses reference cities, which reference states, which reference countries, establishing a full geographic dimension.

7Recommendations

Gaps and questions worth raising with the business.

  • Clarify whether a single Order can have multiple Payments (e.g., partial payments) or only one. The schema allows optional payment, but reconciliation logic should be explicit.

  • Consider whether Return Order should be a line-item-level entity (allowing partial returns of an order item) or remain order-product scoped. The current model does not enforce that the product being returned was in the order being referenced.

  • Establish data quality rules: ensure that every Order has a Customer and every Order Line Item has a Product before going to production, or accept the business implications of null foreign keys.

  • Document the Employee hierarchy rules: can managers form cycles? What is the maximum depth of the reporting structure? Should there be a root executive with no manager?

  • Consider adding a Product-Promotion relationship end-date or validity period if promotions are time-bound campaigns.

  • Audit Log is disconnected from the main data model. Decide whether to link it to affected entities (e.g., Order audit logs) or keep it as a general system log.

  • Review Inventory reorder_level: the sample shows a single value (20) across all rows. Confirm whether this is a real constant or a data artifact; if variable, ensure reorder logic is enforced in business processes.

  • Ensure Order status values (e.g., 'DELIVERED') and Payment method values (e.g., 'UPI') are validated against a controlled vocabulary list documented in a separate configuration or reference table.

  • Validate whether Product status and Customer status should be linked or are independent state machines.

  • Confirm whether Shipment is always initiated from a single Warehouse or whether split shipments from multiple warehouses for one order are possible; the current schema allows only one warehouse per shipment.
